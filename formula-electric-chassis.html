Team Phoenix Racing (Phoenix Racing Electric), SVNIT Surat

Designing and building a Formula Electric chassis

The chassis of Team Phoenix Racing’s Formula Student electric car. As chassis head, I led its design and fabrication with a 30-member team, optimizing the frame with finite element analysis to cut mass while meeting its stiffness target. Competing as Phoenix Racing Electric in Formula Bharat 2023, Class I, the team won the Concept Goals Management event in the electric category.

The problem

The chassis carries every load the car sees, from the suspension and driver to the battery pack, and has to meet the rulebook’s safety requirements and a stiffness target while weighing as little as possible. Every kilogram of frame is acceleration and battery range lost.

What I built

As chassis head, I led a 30-member team through the design and fabrication of the chassis. I ran finite element analysis on the frame and removed material where it wasn’t needed, iterating until it met the stiffness target at lower mass. I also calculated the suspension hardpoints, optimized the vehicle dynamics, balanced the weight distribution and driver ergonomics, and developed the bodywork and aero package alongside performance simulations. In 2023, I mentored juniors on the team’s design and development for the next competition.

How it works

Testing and results

The frame was checked in FEA against the stiffness target at every iteration. The team then competed as Phoenix Racing Electric (team EA05) in Formula Bharat 2023, Class I, held virtually from October 7 to 29, 2022, and won the Concept Goals Management event in the electric category.

Why it matters

A lighter frame that still meets its stiffness target makes the whole car faster. Leading the chassis team also meant making engineering trade-offs under a competition deadline and rulebook, while keeping a 30-person team working toward the same goal.

Certificate

Technical notes

Why stiffness matters

A stiff frame lets the suspension, not the chassis, control how the tires load the track. If the frame twists, the car’s handling becomes harder to predict and tune, which is why torsional stiffness is a standard check for a race-car frame.

Reducing mass with FEA

Analysis shows which parts of the frame carry little load. Those can be lightened or removed, then the frame is checked again, until mass is down and the stiffness target is still met.
