About me

Mechanical engineer by training, roboticist by choice.

I got into robotics by building things that had to work: a race-car chassis that had to survive a season, a spraying robot that had to find its own way down a crop row, a drone whose rotors all ran off one engine. Each project pushed me from mechanical design toward the control and planning problems that decide whether a machine actually does its job.

At CMU I work on on-orbit servicing research funded by SURI: how a free-flying robot can rendezvous with a tumbling satellite, grasp it, and stop its spin, including with Snaker, our snake robot. I’ve worked on the motion planning, the controllers, the gripper design, and the grasp analysis, and I’m now building an early tendon-driven version of Snaker with two teammates. I’m currently a course assistant for Machine Learning and AI for Engineers, and previously assisted AI Agents for Engineers and Applied Finite Element Analysis.

I graduate in May 2027 and am looking for a full-time role in robotic manipulation, controls, or motion planning, ideally on a team that tests its ideas on physical robots.

On-orbit servicing research at CMU, funded by SURIAug 2025 – present

Capturing and detumbling a tumbling satellite

Most satellites were never designed to be grabbed, and a failed one is often tumbling. Our CMU team built a simulated pipeline in which a free-flying robot reaches a tumbling satellite, grasps it, and stops its spin, plus Snaker, a snake robot that wraps around features a standard gripper can’t hold. Our planner reached the moving grasp point in 99% of randomized trials, and Snaker tolerated up to five times more misalignment than a standard gripper. We’ve also started building a tendon-driven Snaker in hardware, at a very early stage, and showed in SOFA simulations that tapering its spine keeps its loops even. I worked on every stage: planning, control, gripper design, and grasp analysis.

Engine-powered drone, SVNIT SuratJan – Jun 2024

Driving every rotor from a single engine

One engine powers all four propellers of this drone. A conventional drivetrain would lock every propeller to a fixed speed ratio, so I designed a contact-free magnetic gear pair for each propeller: a servo-driven linkage changes the gap between the two gears, which sets how much power, and so how much speed, reaches that propeller. The feedback control improved stability in flight tests.

Course projects

24-774 Advanced Control System IntegrationFall 2026 · In progress

Rotor fault detection and recovery on a Crazyflie

A team project to let a Crazyflie 2.1 nano-quadrotor detect and survive partial thrust loss on any rotor without motor-speed sensors. My proposal compares the IMU-measured forces and torques with the commanded motor outputs, estimates each rotor’s health with recursive least squares, flags a steady mismatch with a CUSUM test, and reallocates thrust, giving up yaw to keep altitude, roll, and pitch.

  • Fault detection
  • State estimation
  • Control allocation
  • Crazyflie

24-774 Advanced Control System IntegrationFall 2026

Rotary pendulum control on the Quanser QUBE-Servo 2

Team hardware labs on a rotary pendulum. I designed a discrete PID motor-angle controller by pole placement and ran it on an Arduino, tracking a ±10° square wave; a sampling-rate sweep showed instability at 90 ms, close to the model’s prediction of about 98 ms. I also implemented a Luenberger observer on the Arduino, found and fixed an angle-offset error, and estimated both joint speeds with 0.98 and 0.96 correlation to measurements.

  • PID control
  • State observers
  • Arduino
  • MATLAB
  • Simulink

16-782 Planning and Decision-Making in RoboticsFall 2026

Catching a moving target on large cost maps

A C++ planner that intercepts a moving target at minimum path cost on 2-D cost maps of up to 1825 × 2332 cells, within a one-second budget per planning call. It combines breadth-first search for reachability, Dijkstra-based lower bounds and heuristics, and an exact layered search with branch-and-bound pruning, and it reached the proven optimal cost on all nine test maps.

  • C++
  • Graph search
  • Heuristic search
  • Motion planning
