About me

Mechanical engineer by training, roboticist by choice.

I got into robotics by building things that had to work: a race-car chassis that had to survive a season, a spraying robot that had to find its own way down a crop row, a drone whose rotors all ran off one engine. Each project pushed me from mechanical design toward the control and planning problems that decide whether a machine actually does its job.

At CMU I work on on-orbit manipulation research funded by SURI: how a free-flying robot can rendezvous with a tumbling satellite, grasp it, and stop its spin, including with Snaker, our snake robot. I’ve worked on the motion planning, the controllers, the gripper design, and the grasp analysis, and I’m now building an early tendon-driven version of Snaker with two teammates. I’m currently a course assistant for Machine Learning and AI for Engineers, and previously assisted AI Agents for Engineers and Applied Finite Element Analysis.

I graduate in May 2027 and am looking for a full-time role in robotic manipulation, controls, or motion planning, ideally on a team that tests its ideas on physical robots.

Designer and controls developer, SVNIT SuratJan – Jun 2024

Engine-Powered Drone

I designed a contact-free magnetic gear for each propeller of this single-engine drone, the servo linkage that changes the gap between its two gears, and the feedback control that uses that gap to set each propeller’s speed. Running every rotor off one engine lets the drone fly longer in remote areas while staying lighter and cheaper than one carrying large battery packs. At the smallest gap the propeller reached 75% of its ideal speed, and the feedback control improved stability in flight tests.

Prosthetic Foot

I designed a prosthetic foot whose forefoot can tilt from side to side like a human foot’s, taking it through three CAD iterations refined with Ansys FEA, and built a test bed that loads it repeatably. Working with Ronak Vyas under Prof. Vineet Vashista, we cut the forefoot slits from four to two after FEA showed the extra slits only weakened the foot, matched it to a standard pylon mount, and compared carbon fiber, POM, and PPS for a foot meant to weigh 600–700 g and last a million load cycles.

24-677 Modern Control TheoryFall 2025

Control and Estimation for Autonomous Vehicles

I derived a car’s dynamics by hand and wrote its controllers in Python for a simulated Tesla Model 3 in Webots: PID and LQR tracking, A* re-planning to overtake another car, and EKF SLAM. Driving on its own EKF SLAM estimates, the car scored 100/100 on the course autograder. For a DJI Mavic 2 Pro, my model reference adaptive controller kept the drone flying with up to 65% of one motor’s thrust lost, where LQR alone crashed at 50%.

Introduction to Deep LearningSpring 2026

Deep Learning for Engine Remaining-Useful-Life Prediction

With a teammate, I benchmarked an LSTM, a Transformer encoder, and a temporal convolutional network in PyTorch for predicting how many cycles a turbofan engine has left, on NASA’s C-MAPSS data, training each with three seeds under one protocol. The Transformer was most accurate (13.66 ± 0.47 cycles RMSE), a single-seed run would have flipped the top two, and the TCN learned best from little data.

24-774 Advanced Control Systems IntegrationFall 2026

Rotary Pendulum Control on the Quanser QUBE-Servo 2

I designed a discrete PID motor-angle controller by pole placement for our team’s hardware labs and ran it on an Arduino, tracking a ±10° square wave; a sampling-rate sweep showed instability at 90 ms, close to the model’s prediction of about 98 ms. I also implemented a Luenberger observer on the Arduino, found and fixed an angle-offset error, and estimated both joint speeds with 0.98 and 0.96 correlation to measurements.

16-782 Planning and Decision-Making in RoboticsFall 2026

Moving-Target Interception Planner

I wrote a C++ planner that intercepts a moving target at minimum path cost on 2-D cost maps of up to 1825 × 2332 cells, within a one-second budget per planning call. It combines breadth-first search for reachability, Dijkstra-based lower bounds and heuristics, and an exact layered search with branch-and-bound pruning, and it reached the proven optimal cost on all nine test maps.

Rotor Fault Detection and Recovery on a Crazyflie

My proposal for our team project lets a Crazyflie 2.1 nano-quadrotor detect and survive partial thrust loss on any rotor without motor-speed sensors: compare the IMU-measured forces and torques with the commanded motor outputs, estimate each rotor’s health with recursive least squares, flag a steady mismatch with a CUSUM test, and reallocate thrust, giving up yaw to keep altitude, roll, and pitch.
