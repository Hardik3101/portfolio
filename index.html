Mission Robotic Vehicle: grasping in microgravity

Aug 2025 – present

Carnegie Mellon University. My role: controls and motion planning on a multi-person research team.

The problem

Servicing a satellite in orbit, whether refueling it, repairing it, or removing it before it becomes debris, means a free-floating robot has to approach and grasp an object it has never touched. Every contact pushes the vehicle back, the target's state is only partly observable from onboard sensors, and a collision can end the mission.

What I built

I own the controls and motion-planning side of the project. I modeled manipulator–object contact dynamics in MuJoCo under microgravity so the team can study how grasp forces propagate through a free-floating base. I developed perception-aware motion planning that accounts for state uncertainty and partial observability, so approach trajectories keep the target within the sensors' view instead of assuming a perfect state estimate. I also implemented collision-aware control strategies for proximity and grasping operations.

How it is tested

The planner and controllers are evaluated in MuJoCo across randomized target poses and levels of state uncertainty. The project runs through April 2027, and this section will grow as results come in.

Why it matters

On-orbit servicing extends the life of satellites that would otherwise be abandoned and helps clear debris from crowded orbits, which lowers collision risk for every other spacecraft.

RAKSH: an autonomous spraying robot for polyhouses

Feb 2023 – Dec 2023

SVNIT Surat. My role: controls, perception, and team lead for a five-member team.

The problem

Spraying pesticides inside a polyhouse, a plastic-covered greenhouse, is hot, repetitive work that keeps farm workers in close contact with chemicals. A robot that drives itself down the rows and sprays only what is needed takes people out of that exposure and frees their time for work that needs judgment.

What I built

I led a five-person team and owned navigation and perception. I developed the autonomous navigation using sensor fusion and vision-based row detection, so the robot follows crop rows from what its camera sees rather than from a pre-mapped route. I also designed a modular pesticide-delivery system that holds ten chemicals, so one robot can be configured for different crops and treatments.

How it was tested

We took the robot from concept to a working prototype within the year and presented it at Robofest.

Why it matters

Polyhouse farming is labor-intensive and chemical-heavy. Automating the spraying step reduces manual labor and cuts the time workers spend breathing and handling pesticides, which matters most on the small farms that can least afford protective equipment.

Engine-powered drone with a magnetic-gear drivetrain

Jan 2024 – Jun 2024

SVNIT Surat. My role: designer and controls developer.

The problem

Battery-powered multirotors run out of energy in minutes. Fuel has far higher energy density, but a single engine then has to drive several rotors, and conventional gearing adds wear, noise, and failure points. With rotors sharing an engine, the aircraft also cannot balance thrust simply by changing each motor's speed.

What I built

I designed a magnetic-gear power transmission that carries torque from one engine to the rotors through magnetic coupling instead of meshing teeth, which removes tooth wear and lets the drivetrain slip rather than break under a shock load. I then implemented real-time feedback control for thrust balancing so the aircraft holds its attitude as load shifts between rotors.

How it was tested

We tested the drivetrain and controller in flight. The feedback controller measurably improved stability in flight tests, and the magnetic coupling removes the tooth wear that a conventional transmission accumulates.

Why it matters

Longer-endurance drones make aerial work practical over larger areas: crop monitoring, infrastructure inspection, and search and rescue all run into the battery limit first.

A high-dexterity prosthetic foot and its test bed

Jan 2025 – Jun 2025

IIT Gandhinagar. My role: project assistant; designed the foot and built the test bed.

The problem

Most affordable prosthetic feet are rigid. Ankle and toe motion lets a wearer handle slopes and uneven ground with less effort and fewer falls, but adding that dexterity adds parts, weight, and cost, and every new design has to be evaluated before it goes anywhere near a person.

What I built

I designed a high-dexterity prosthetic foot, and I designed and built a test bed for evaluating it. The rig applies repeatable loading to a mounted foot, so candidate designs can be compared under the same conditions rather than in one-off trials.

How it was tested

The test bed characterizes each foot under controlled loading and lets the team compare candidate designs on the same rig before committing to user trials, which are slow and hard to repeat.

Why it matters

A foot that adapts to terrain without a large price increase widens access to better mobility, especially where high-end prosthetics are out of reach.

Formula Electric chassis: lighter without losing stiffness

Nov 2021 – Jan 2023

Team Phoenix Racing, SVNIT Surat. My role: chassis head, leading a 30-member team.

The problem

The chassis carries every load the car sees, from suspension and driver to battery pack, and has to meet the rulebook's safety requirements and a torsional-stiffness target while weighing as little as possible. Every kilogram of frame is a kilogram of acceleration and battery range lost.

What I built

As chassis head I owned the frame from concept through manufacturing. I ran finite-element analysis on the governing load cases and used the results to remove material where stress was low, iterating until the design met the stiffness constraint at lower mass. I also coordinated a 30-member team across design, manufacturing, and testing.

How it was tested

The frame was validated in FEA against the stiffness target and then proved in competition: the team placed third in the 2022–23 season.

Why it matters

This project taught me to make engineering trade-offs under a deadline and a rulebook, and to lead a large team through design reviews, manufacturing, and competition, which is the experience Formula-style teams are valued for in robotics hiring.
