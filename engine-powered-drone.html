Engine-powered drone, SVNIT Surat

Driving every rotor from a single engine

One engine powers all four propellers of this drone, which is built for long flights in remote places. I designed a contact-free magnetic gear for each propeller whose gap can be changed: the closer the two gears, the more power passes through, so each propeller’s speed can be controlled even though they all share one engine. The feedback control improved stability in flight tests, and a patent application has been filed for the transmission.

The problem

Battery-powered drones run out of energy quickly, and flying longer means carrying large, heavy, expensive battery packs. An engine carries far more energy in its fuel, but a drone flies by running each propeller at its own speed, and a conventional drivetrain from one engine fixes the speed ratio to every propeller when it is designed.

What I built

Each propeller has its own pair of magnetic gears: one on the shaft driven by the engine and one on the propeller shaft. The gears never touch, so nothing wears. With the two gears at their closest, the most power passes through; widening the gap passes less. A servo drives a linkage that sets the gap, an encoder measures the propeller’s speed, and an Arduino drove the servo and read the encoder. To calibrate it, we held the engine at 3,000 rpm, so with the 2:1 gear the ideal propeller speed was 6,000 rpm, and measured propeller speed as we closed the gap from 10 mm. At 10 mm the propeller didn’t turn; speed then rose roughly exponentially and reached about 4,500 rpm, 75% of ideal, at the smallest gap. Interpolating the readings gave a continuous map of the gap needed for any propeller speed.

How it works

Testing and results

We calibrated each gear pair by measuring propeller speed at a series of gaps with the engine held at 3,000 rpm; at the smallest gap the propeller reached about 75% of its ideal speed. We then tested the drivetrain and controller in flight, where the feedback control improved the drone’s stability. Because the gears never touch, the transmission also avoids the tooth wear a conventional gearbox accumulates.

Why it matters

The drone is meant for long flights in remote locations. Running on fuel lets it fly longer while staying lighter and cheaper than a drone carrying large battery packs, without giving up agility or stability. Crop monitoring, infrastructure inspection, and search and rescue all run into the battery limit first.

Technical notes

How a magnetic gear works

A magnetic gear transmits torque through the pull between magnets rather than through teeth in contact, so nothing wears, and it slips instead of breaking when it is overloaded. That pull weakens as the magnets move apart, which is what lets the gap control how much power gets through.

Why one engine needs adjustable gears

A drone steers and balances by running its propellers at different speeds. With one engine and fixed gearing, every propeller would turn at a set ratio to the engine; an adjustable gap gives each propeller its own speed.
